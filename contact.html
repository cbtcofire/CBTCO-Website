Common questions

Before you call

How do I know if I have a backflow preventer?

If your property has a fire sprinkler system, an irrigation system, or a commercial water service, you almost certainly have at least one. They're usually found in the riser room, a mechanical room, or an outdoor enclosure near the water meter — a brass or bronze assembly with two shutoff valves and test ports. Your water purveyor's notice letter will also list the devices on record.

What happens if my backflow device fails the test?

A failed test isn't reported as a violation — it just means the device needs repair before a passing report can be filed. We document what failed, quote the repair, and in most cases rebuild the assembly with manufacturer parts and retest it. Once it passes, we file the report with your water purveyor.

Will testing interrupt water service or set off the fire alarm?

Backflow testing requires briefly shutting the valves on the assembly, which interrupts flow through that line for a few minutes. Fire system testing can activate alarm signals — we coordinate with your monitoring company to place the account on test before we start, so the fire department isn't dispatched.

Can you take over inspections from our previous vendor?

Yes. Send us your most recent inspection reports or tags if you have them; if not, we'll survey the property, build a device and system inventory, and set up a fresh compliance schedule from there.

Do you handle multiple locations under one account?

Yes — multi-site scheduling is a core part of what we do. We maintain a single tracked schedule across your locations, batch nearby sites to reduce trip charges, and provide consolidated reporting formatted to match your work order or invoicing system.
