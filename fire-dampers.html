Fire Protection

Fire & Smoke Dampers

Location, access, operation, and drop testing of fire and smoke dampers on the required cycle — documented damper by damper with photos, for one of the most commonly missed requirements in commercial buildings.

The hidden requirement

Fire protection you can't see from the hallway

Fire dampers live inside your ductwork at fire-rated walls and floors, where they're supposed to slam shut and stop fire from riding the HVAC system through the building. Because they're invisible in daily operation, they're the requirement most buildings have never tested — many owners don't know how many dampers they have, or where.

The code cycle is one year after installation, then every four years (six for some healthcare occupancies). Each damper must be located, accessed, drop-tested, and returned to service — and each gets its own documented result with photos.

Scope

What's included

  • Damper location survey — building a complete inventory from plans and field inspection
  • Access verification, with access panel installation quoted where none exists
  • Drop testing of fire dampers: fusible link removed, full closure confirmed, damper reset
  • Smoke & combination dampers cycled via actuator with end-switch verification
  • Photo documentation of each damper, before and after
  • Deficiency reporting: seized dampers, missing links, blocked closure, damaged frames
Good to know

Common questions

We've never had our dampers inspected. Where do we even start?

You're in the majority. We start with a survey — plans plus field verification — to build the damper inventory, then test on the code cycle from there. The first round takes the longest; after that it's routine.

What if a damper has no access panel?

It's common, and it's a deficiency itself — an untestable damper is an unmaintainable one. We document it and quote access panel installation so the damper can be tested and serviced going forward.

Will testing shut down our HVAC?

Individual dampers are tested with their duct section's airflow considered; most buildings see no meaningful disruption. For sensitive areas we coordinate timing with your facilities team.
