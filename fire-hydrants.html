Fire Protection

Private Fire Hydrants

Annual inspection, flushing, and lubrication of privately owned hydrants — the part of the water supply most property owners don't realize they're responsible for.

Whose hydrant is it?

If it's on your property, it's probably yours

Hydrants on private property — serving a campus, a shopping center, an industrial site, an apartment complex — typically belong to the property owner, not the city. That means the annual inspection, flushing, and maintenance is the owner's responsibility, and a seized or dry hydrant is the owner's liability.

The failure mode is quiet: a hydrant can stand looking perfect for a decade while its stem seizes and its barrel fills with debris. Annual operation and flushing is what keeps it ready for the one night it matters.

Scope

What's included

  • Annual full-open operation and flushing of each private hydrant
  • Stem, cap, and thread lubrication so caps come off when the fire department needs them
  • Flow observation and notes on pressure or discharge anomalies
  • Drainage check — barrels that don't drain freeze and crack
  • Condition report with photos: caps, threads, paint, accessibility, and clearance
  • Repair quotes for seized stems, missing caps, damaged threads, or obstructed access
Good to know

Common questions

How do I know if a hydrant is private or city-owned?

Generally, hydrants on your side of the property line and fed from your private fire main are yours. Site plans and the water purveyor's records settle it — we can help you sort out which hydrants you own during the first visit.

What happens if a hydrant fails inspection?

You get a written report of exactly what's wrong — seized stem, bad threads, poor drainage — with photos and a repair quote. Nothing is repaired without your approval.

Do hydrant flushings make a mess?

Flushing discharges a significant volume of water briefly. We direct flow away from landscaping and traffic, and schedule around your site's activity when discharge location is sensitive.
