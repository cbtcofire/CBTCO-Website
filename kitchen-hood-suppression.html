Fire Protection

Kitchen Hood Suppression

Semi-annual inspection of wet chemical suppression systems in commercial kitchens — nozzles, fusible links, pull stations, and gas shutoff verified, scheduled around your kitchen's operating hours.

The highest-risk room in the building

Grease fires don't wait for business hours

Cooking equipment is the leading cause of commercial building fires, and the wet chemical system over your line is the defense built for exactly that fire. Code requires inspection every six months because the system lives in a hostile environment — heat, grease, and vibration degrade links, clog nozzles, and shift pipe.

We work around service hours — early mornings before prep or after close — so the inspection never costs you a lunch rush.

Scope

What's included

  • Semi-annual inspection of wet chemical suppression systems
  • Nozzle inspection: caps in place, aim verified against current appliance layout
  • Fusible link replacement on the required schedule
  • Manual pull station and automatic actuation verification
  • Gas shutoff & electrical interlock testing
  • System tagging and documentation for the fire marshal and your insurance carrier
Good to know

Common questions

Do we have to shut the kitchen down for the inspection?

The inspection is performed with the cooking line down, but we schedule before prep or after close so it doesn't cost you service hours. A typical system takes about an hour.

We rearranged our cooking line — does that matter?

Very much. Nozzle placement is engineered to the specific appliances beneath them. Moving a fryer under a nozzle aimed for a griddle can leave it unprotected — tell us about layout changes and we'll verify or re-aim coverage.

Is hood cleaning the same thing as suppression inspection?

No — duct and hood cleaning is a separate service from suppression system inspection, though both are code requirements. We inspect the suppression system; if grease buildup is compromising it, that gets flagged in your report.
